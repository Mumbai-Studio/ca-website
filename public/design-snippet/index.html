Is this where you belong?

Thank you for your interest in joining Collaborative Architecture.

Headquartered in Mumbai, with a studio in Riyadh, KSA, Collaborative Architecture is an international practice driven by the convergence of people, places, cultures, and interdisciplinary thinking.

We are interested in individuals who are curious, intellectually adventurous, creatively restless, and willing to challenge conventions.

A few things we look for

  1. 01

    Digital fluency

    A good command of Rhino and Grasshopper is a definite advantage. Grasshopper ninjas are particularly welcome! If your skills are still developing but you are willing to learn, we are happy to take a chance.

  2. 02

    Intellectual curiosity

    We expect you to read, explore, question, and engage with ideas beyond architecture.

  3. 03

    A sense of humor

    Being a real joker helps. A stand-up comedian would fit right in.

  4. 04

    Independent thinking

    We value lateral thinkers, humanists, interdisciplinary minds, and people with an unusually broad spectrum of interests.

  5. 05

    Architectural awareness

    Familiarity with significant architects, designers, thinkers, and their work will certainly help during the interview.

Your university grades?
Not particularly interested.
Your ideas? Very much so.

More than a place to work

Collaborative is a studio where you are expected to challenge yourself, challenge us, and explore possibilities beyond the obvious.

We are not particularly interested in conventional job seekers. We look for people with the curiosity to question, the courage to experiment, and the imagination to make their own rules.

Our work culture can be intense, unconventional, and occasionally intimidating. For some, it might seem demanding; for others, liberating.

We are actively looking for the latter.

01·A

The Rods

Fifty rods. Create anything with them.

You have

Create anything with it, but

  • Use every rod. Cut none.
  • It stands on its own.
  • Someone can sit on it, in it or under it.

The model

  • Model it in Rhino.
  • If you can, build it in Grasshopper, with one slider that changes the design.
  • Show it from eye level, the way someone would first meet it.
01·B

The Screen

One sheet of plywood, perforated.

You haveOne sheet, 1220 × 2440 mm, 12 mm thick.

The screen

  • Holes from 20 to 120 mm across.
  • At least 25 mm of material between holes, and 60 mm at the edges.
  • The pattern responds to one thing you choose: a point, a line or a photograph.

The script

  • A Grasshopper definition draws the pattern. Move your point, or change your photograph, and it updates.
  • It reports the number of holes and how much of the sheet is open.
  • Draw the sheet flat, ready to cut.

02·A

The Kit

Create anything with it. Use every piece.

You have

Create anything with it, but

  • Use every piece. Cut none.
  • It touches the ground at no more than three points.
  • One person can stand upright inside or under it (2100 mm clear).
  • Every rod meets at least two other pieces.

The script

  • One Grasshopper definition builds the whole thing.
  • A single slider must give at least three different versions that still obey all four rules.
  • A panel shows the live count of each piece used.
02·B

The Field

A wall 6000 mm long and 3000 mm high.

The point: a person walking pastThe curve: anything you want it to be450 pieces of 200 mm, one way of many

The wall

  • Build it from one repeated component no bigger than 300 × 300 × 300 mm. Minimum 400 pieces.
  • The component changes (turn, opening, depth, your call) in response to two things: a point, which is a person walking past, and a curve, which is anything you want it to be.
  • Somewhere the wall is fully closed. Somewhere it is at least 70% open.

The script

  • Move the point or redraw the curve and the wall updates.
  • Every component is numbered.
  • All pieces are laid flat for cutting from 1220 × 2440 mm sheets, and the script reports how many sheets you need.

03·A

The Canopy

A canopy 8 × 8 m, cut from flat sheets.

You have18 mm birch plywood in 1220 × 2440 mm sheets, a CNC router with a 6 mm cutter, and M8 bolts, as many as you need.

The canopy

  • It covers 8 × 8 m on plan, with at least 2400 mm clear below, on no more than three supports.
  • Every part is cut flat from one sheet. At least 150 parts.
  • Parts join by slots, notches or bolts. No glue, no nails.
  • The nest uses at least 70% of each sheet.

The script

  • Every part carries an ID that says where it sits. The script engraves it on the part and prints it on the drawings.
  • It nests all parts 6 mm apart and reports the sheets used and the yield.
  • Change the canopy’s form and the parts, IDs, nest and drawings all update.
03·B

The Facade

A west facade in Riyadh, 30 m long and 12 m high.

You haveA wall 30 × 12 m facing west in Riyadh (24.7° N), a panel module of your choice, and at least 600 panels.

The facade

  • Each panel opens or closes in response to the sun on that wall (Ladybug, or your own calculation).
  • No more than 12 panel types. Every panel within 5 mm of its ideal shape, or a tolerance you justify.
  • The data tree has one branch per floor and one item per panel: {floor;bay}.

The script

  • It writes a panel schedule (CSV): ID, type, position, rotation and deviation.
  • It rebuilds the whole facade from that CSV alone.
  • It reports how many panels of each type, and what a thirteenth type would save.

Send us, for each snippet

Level 01Interns
  1. Two or three images: renders, or photographs of a model you made.
  2. The Rhino file (.3dm), and the .gh file if you made one.
  3. A hand sketch, if you have one. We like sketches.
  4. 100 words on the idea.

We look at the idea, the eye and how clearly you show it. A simple model with a clear idea beats a complicated one with none.

Level 02Junior Architects
  1. Three 3D images.
  2. The .gh file with all geometry internalized, and your Rhino version.
  3. Native components preferred. If you use plug-ins, list them.
  4. 100 words on the idea.

We will be evaluating both the 3D and the script. A beautiful model with a messy script loses to a good model with a script we can read.

Level 03Advanced
  1. Three 3D images.
  2. The fabrication set. The Canopy: one DXF per sheet (cut, engrave and drill layers), the assembly sequence in 6 to 10 steps, and the parts schedule (CSV). The Facade: every panel type at 1:20 with dimensions, and the panel schedule (CSV).
  3. The .gh file with all geometry internalized and its groups named, and your Rhino version.
  4. A diagram of your data tree: what each path holds.
  5. Plug-ins are welcome (Karamba3D, Ladybug, OpenNest, your own). List them.
  6. 150 words on the idea.
